PHASE 8 -- GRID RISK DESIGN EXPERIMENT
====================================================================================================
Locked pipeline reproduced verbatim from the approved Phase 7 script.
Only the Grid Stress Score formula is varied. Model, features, test period, and
band thresholds are unchanged. No threshold is tuned around any single day.

PRE-FLIGHT CHECK: known season peak 8,392.6 MW at 2025-06-12 23:00:00 is present in the test set. Actual value at that timestamp: 8,392.6 MW.
Locked pipeline reproduced 5,817 test blocks, 69,148 train blocks -- identical counts to Phase 7.

====================================================================================================
SECTION 1 -- CANDIDATE FORMULATIONS
====================================================================================================
All three formulations share the identical non-utilisation terms:
  growth    = clip(day-over-day change in forecast (P50) peak / 0.08, 0, 1)   weight 10
  heat      = clip((corrected forecast temp - 30.0) / (12.0), 0, 1)   weight 10
  unreliab. = HIGH 0.0, MEDIUM 0.5, LOW 1.0                                          weight 5
  u(x) = clip((x/capacity - 0.7) / 0.30, 0, 1)   (same utilisation mapping for both quantiles)

The ONLY thing that changes is how the fixed 75-point utilisation budget is split between
u(P50) and u(P90):

  formulation                               w(P50)    w(P90)   w(P50)+w(P90)
  A -- P50-driven (current, Phase 7)          55.0      20.0            75.0
  B -- P90-driven                             20.0      55.0            75.0
  C -- Combined (equal split)                 37.5      37.5            75.0

  A treats the point forecast (expected demand) as the primary risk driver and the upper band
    only as a secondary check -- this is what Phase 7 shipped and what triggered this review.
  B treats the upper-risk exposure (P90) as the primary driver and the point forecast as secondary.
  C is a genuine combination: P50 and P90 contribute equally, so the score reflects both
    'what we expect' and 'how bad it could plausibly get' at the same time.

  Band thresholds are IDENTICAL and untouched across all three: 0-50 NORMAL, 50-75 WATCH,
  75-90 HIGH, 90-100 CRITICAL. No threshold is retuned to make any formulation, or any single
  day, land in a particular band.

====================================================================================================
SECTION 2 -- METHOD
====================================================================================================
For each of the 61 test days we compute, once, the shared raw components (u(P50), u(P90),
growth, heat, reliability penalty) from the locked pipeline's outputs -- these do not depend
on the formulation. We then score each day three times, once per formulation, using only the
declared weight split above. Nothing else differs between A/B/C.

Evaluation criteria (fixed in advance, none of them tuned to 12 June specifically):
  1. Correlation between stress_score and REALIZED daily peak utilisation (actual peak / capacity)
     across all 61 test days -- does the score track what actually happened, on average?
  2. False-alarm rate: share of days with actual utilisation < 80% (comfortably below capacity)
     that are scored WATCH or above.
  3. Missed-flag rate: share of days with actual utilisation >= 90% (close to or above the
     illustrative ceiling) that are scored NORMAL (no flag at all).
  4. Behaviour on 11-13 June 2025 specifically -- the case that triggered this review -- shown
     for context, NOT used to pick the winner.
  5. Behaviour on a broader, non-cherry-picked set of days where P50 and P90 diverge widely
     (uncertainty was large) versus days where they are close (uncertainty was small).

====================================================================================================
SECTION 7 -- FINAL CHOICE
====================================================================================================
  Highest rank-correlation with realized daily utilisation: B -- P90-driven (Spearman 0.836).

  HONEST RESULT FIRST, before any recommendation: under the UNCHANGED band thresholds, 12 June
  (and 13 June, also 94% P90 utilisation) stays in WATCH under all three formulations -- A, B,
  and C. None of them promotes it to HIGH. Changing which quantile drives the score narrows the
  understatement, it does not eliminate the categorical result the review was concerned about:
    12 June stress score:  A 58.3  ->  B 66.4  ->  C 62.4   (all three: WATCH, band edge is 75)
  Closing that categorical gap would require moving the WATCH/HIGH threshold or widening the
  70-100% utilisation mapping range -- both are explicitly OUT OF SCOPE for this experiment
  ("do NOT optimize thresholds specifically around 12 June"), so this report does not attempt it.

  What DOES measurably change between formulations, across all 61 test days (not just 12 June):
    - rank-correlation with realized outcomes: A 0.796 < C 0.822 < B 0.836
    - false-alarm rate on comfortable days (actual < 80% capacity, n=46): A 0.0%, B 2.2%, C 0.0%
    - B's higher correlation comes with the only nonzero false-alarm rate observed (1 of 46
      comfortable days scored WATCH); A and C stay at zero false alarms in this window.

  RECOMMENDATION: adopt C -- COMBINED (equal 37.5/37.5 split of the utilisation budget) as the
  proposed direction, WITH the limitation above stated plainly alongside it.
  Reasoning:
    - C captures most of B's correlation gain (0.822 vs 0.836, vs A's 0.796) while matching A's
      zero false-alarm rate in this test window -- B trades a small amount of extra correlation
      for the only real false alarm any formulation produced here.
    - C narrows the specific understatement that triggered this review (12 June moves from 58.3
      to 62.4, more than a third of the way from A's score to B's) without adopting B's more
      one-sided reliance on the (less certain) upper quantile.
    - Section 5 confirms the three formulations only diverge on days where P50 and P90 actually
      disagree, and agree closely when they don't -- so adopting C does not change behaviour on
      the ordinary majority of days.
    - This narrows, but by itself does NOT resolve, the original finding -- see the honest result
      above. Fully resolving it is a threshold/mapping question this experiment was told not to
      answer, and should be raised as its own explicit decision in a future phase.

  This choice is PROPOSED, not yet adopted, and is presented for approval like every other phase
  in this project.

====================================================================================================
SECTION 8 -- LIMITATIONS
====================================================================================================
  - 61 test days is a small sample for a 4-band, 0-100 index; the CRITICAL band (>=90) is not
    exercised by any formulation in this window, so its behaviour there remains unvalidated.
  - The utilisation mapping u(x) and the 70-100% mapping range, the growth/heat weights, and the
    HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW reliability penalties (0 / 0.5 / 1.0) were NOT part of this experiment -- only
    the P50/P90 split was varied. Those other choices carry the same caveats noted in Phase 7.
  - The false-alarm and missed-flag thresholds used to evaluate the formulations (80% / 90% actual
    utilisation) are themselves judgement calls, not derived from any labeled ground truth of what
    counts as a 'real' grid-stress event -- there is no such label in this dataset.
  - All three formulations still ultimately rest on the same illustrative, unverified 9,000 MW
    capacity figure; a different capacity assumption could change which formulation looks best.
  - peak_risk (the separate categorical field, already P90-anchored since Phase 7) is unchanged
    and out of scope for this experiment -- only stress_score/stress_level were varied.
  - This is a design comparison on historical data, not an operational validation; none of the
    proposed bands or the chosen formulation have been tested against real dispatcher outcomes.
